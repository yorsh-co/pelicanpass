# Event report: 17 October 2026

A post-event technical report for the one-day conference held on 17 October 2026. It sets
what the design and the load test predicted beside what the live event showed, reports the
incidents with timelines, and says where the data cannot answer.

It is a read of the operational record, not a retrospective on the product. Where a number is
missing, it is marked unknown rather than estimated.

## Summary

The application held the event. No request was refused for load, no server error was logged
during the event, and the participant check-in numbers run from 1 to 528 with no gap and no
duplicate.

The record is not clean. Three things went wrong or were not understood in time:

- **The 19:00 deploy** stopped the database and the cache together with the app. Three requests
  were answered `503`, one telemetry batch was lost, and the shutdown alert was not delivered.
- **Both "Queue at the door" alerts were correct on their definition and wrong in their
  conclusion.** The rule fired because attendees finished the QR step on arrival, not because
  anything was down.
- **The event-day request log does not exist.** The traffic summary was taken at teardown, after
  every container that served the event had been recreated. Request volume, requests per minute,
  latency and limiter headroom for 17/10 are therefore unknown.

## Predicted and observed

### Capacity

The load test measured server capacity on `prod-server` at the 1.1.0 release candidate on 14/10
([`load-test-results.md`](../performance/load-test-results.md),
[README](../../README.md#capacity)).

| Phase  | Comfortable | Ceiling     | Note                                       |
| ------ | ----------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Raffle | 3,500 users | 4,000 users | The binding phase: one core sets the limit |
| Survey | -           | ≥ 10,000    | No knee up to the highest step tested      |

The room was 528 participants and 119 volunteers checked in. The prediction was that the host
would carry that many times over.

The event neither confirms nor refutes this, and the reason matters. The test counts requests
from simulated phones. The event data counts check-ins, and a check-in is not a request: each
takes several requests across the ticket, verification and QR steps, plus polling. The record
that would let us compare request rates, the event-day request log, was lost (see
[What the data cannot show](#what-the-data-cannot-show)).

What the data does support is narrower:

- No `5xx` was logged during the event. The three `503` responses fall in the 19:00 deploy window
  and come from the shutdown, not from load.
- The log has no "Load shedding started" line, so shedding never engaged. This is consistent with
  the test, which predicted that nothing in [overload behaviour](../performance/overload-behaviour.md)
  engages at the event's own load. Only warn-level and above is exported, so the absence of an
  info-level line is not proof on its own. The absence of any `503` outside the deploy is.
- The peak of arrivals was modest against the test: 17 check-ins in the busiest minute.

### Rate limiting

The design assumes hundreds of people behind one venue address. The strict per-address quotas are
sized for a carrier NAT, and the venue address is meant to sit in `TRUSTED_IPS`, which raises the
quota three- to tenfold on the limiters that matter ([`rate-limiting.md`](../performance/rate-limiting.md)).

| Limiter               | Strict | Trusted |
| --------------------- | ------ | ------- |
| `rl:ip`               | 300/60 | 2000/60 |
| `rl:ip:web`           | 300/60 | 1000/60 |
| `rl:ip:checkin-entry` | 60/60  | 600/60  |

What happened:

- The operator forgot the venue address and added it later in the morning. The app-only restart
  at 11:06 is the matching recreate: the container changes and no Redis or Postgres error appears
  around it. `TRUSTED_IPS` is read at process start, so the change took effect then.
- No `429` was recorded on 17/10, in the warn-level log or in `app_events`. The only `429`s in
  the whole record are 25 on `POST /api/v1/checkin/ticket` on 15/10, two days before the event.
  Their context is not in the data.
- Participant check-in opened at 11:30, after the change. Volunteers checked in from 08:40 under
  the strict tier.

What this cannot confirm. A `429` is always logged, so zero `429` means none was emitted. It does
not mean the limiters were far from their quotas, because successful requests are not logged and
per-address counts do not exist. The data cannot say how many volunteers shared an address, so it
cannot say whether the strict tier was ever close to binding in the morning. It also cannot show
that adding the address mattered, since neither side of the change has a `429` to compare.

The omission is a process failure, not a design failure. The design names the step; nothing
checked that it had been done (see [Follow-ups](#follow-ups)).

### Many attendees were already checked in at home

Attendees did the ticket and verification steps at home and finished the QR step on arrival. The
check-in session lasts 90 minutes. The curve above is therefore QR completion, not arrival at the
venue, and not the earlier steps. The log and `app_events` record only failed requests, so the
per-step funnel is not recoverable.

### The two "Queue at the door" alerts

The rule is in [`rules.ts`](../../src/shared/telemetry/rules.ts). Every 30 seconds, over the last
three minutes of in-process counters, it fires when `checkin.req` is at least 30 and
`checkin.complete` is 0. `checkin.req` counts every request to `/checkin*` and
`/api/v1/checkin/*`, whatever the status. `checkin.complete` counts only the final QR step. Its
text says the room is filling up and that the cause is almost always Redis.

| Alert | Requests counted | Completions counted | Window |
| ----- | ---------------- | ------------------- | ------ |
| 11:35 | 49               | 0                   | 3 min  |
| 11:51 | 39               | 0                   | 3 min  |

The second also carried one suppressed repeat.

For the timing: the WhatsApp day message ran from 11:22 (the operator recalls about
11:20), participant check-in opened at 11:30, and arrival was around 12:00.

What the data shows:

- The roster agrees with both alerts. It has zero completions in the three minutes ending at each
  alert, and completions on both sides of each window.
- At least 46 of the 49 and all 39 counted requests were not logged as errors, so they were
  `2xx` or `3xx`. The few failures in the windows were validation errors on a wrong code.
- No `5xx`, no `429` and no Redis or Postgres error appears between 11:06 and 18:59.

So the door was working. People were opening the page and doing the early steps, and nobody was
yet scanning the QR.

The rule did what it was written to do. Its conclusion was wrong for this pattern. "Requests
without completions" was meant to separate a stalled door from a quiet one, and it assumed that
requests mean people at the door. With the first two steps done at home, that assumption fails
exactly in the minutes after check-in opens. This is a lesson about alert semantics: a rule's
message should claim what the signal proves, which is a pause in completions, not a full room and
not Redis. The data cannot prove the at-home explanation; it only fits it.

### Door messages

The door message goes out when an operator adds an attendee at the door. It is sent by
[`admin.whatsapp-door.service.ts`](../../src/modules/admin/admin.whatsapp-door.service.ts) and
the client in [`whatsapp.client.ts`](../../src/modules/whatsapp/whatsapp.client.ts).

Between 12:05 and 13:53 there were 26 failed sends, each logged as a pair: `whatsapp send failed`
from the client and `Door message not sent` from the service.

| Cause                               | Count |
| ----------------------------------- | ----- |
| `ETIMEDOUT` to the Graph API        | 23    |
| `EAI_AGAIN` resolving the Graph API | 3     |

None carried a Meta error code. Both causes mean the request never got an answer. No such lines
appear on other days.

What the path does when a send fails:

1. The client catches the timeout or dropped connection, logs it, and returns a failed outcome
   with no code.
2. The service marks the reservation row `failed` and logs `Door message not sent`.
3. The call returns `{ status: 'failed' }`. There is no automatic retry.
4. A `failed` row does not count as a sent number or against the per-attendee cap of 3, so a
   later save for the same attendee can send again. That is a manual retry, not a recovery.

The count is a numerator without a denominator. Successful door sends are info-level, which the
export does not keep, and the `whatsapp_door_sends` table was not exported. How many attendees
were added at the door, and how many of those never received a message, is unknown.

Attendee check-in was not affected in the log: none of the Turnstile failures is on a check-in
path. The door messages were.

The v1.1.1 deploy at about 19:00 stopped more than the app. The log shows Postgres terminating
the app's connection by administrator command (`57P01`) at 18:59:57, and `cache` failing DNS
resolution (`EAI_AGAIN`) at 19:00:02. So `db` and `cache` were stopped alongside `app`. The
operator does not remember the command that was run, so the mechanism is not stated here.

| Time     | Event                                                                 |
| -------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 18:59:57 | Postgres terminates the app's connection (`57P01`).                   |
| 18:59:59 | Two `503`s: `POST /api/v1/attendee/logout` and `GET /checkin`.        |
| 19:00:01 | The shutdown alert to Discord times out.                              |

The consequences were three requests answered `503`, one lost telemetry batch of 2 rows, a failed

The new container's first record is at 19:02. The `503` window was under ten seconds. The shutdown path under lost dependencies is a gap worth

### Why the request log is gone

This is a runbook gap, not an accident. The teardown procedure
([`deployment.md`](../operations/deployment.md)) captures the traffic summary from the container's
standard output, because the request log is the only record of successful requests. Standard
output lives only as long as the container. Every recreate after the event discarded it: the 19:00
deploy on 17/10, and the restarts on 18/10. By teardown, the log started at 14:02 on 18/10.

The traffic summary therefore covers 18/10 14:02 to 19/10 08:56 and describes no part of the event.
It must not be quoted as event load. The runbook assumed the container would live from the event
to the teardown, and nothing enforced that assumption.

## Follow-ups

No code was changed for this report. Each item says where it lives.

1. **Capture the traffic summary before any post-event recreate.** Run the teardown capture
   immediately after the event, and before any deploy or restart. Runbook
   ([`deployment.md`](../operations/deployment.md)).
2. **Persist the request log across recreates.** The summary needs the event-day log, so keep it
   on a volume or ship it off the container. Runbook and compose configuration.
3. **Deploy `app` only during an event.** Never stop `db` or `cache` while attendees are active,
   and rehearse what shutdown does when its dependencies disappear: the 19:00 shutdown timed out
   and lost a batch. Runbook, then code if the rehearsal shows a gap.
4. **Reword or rework the "Queue at the door" rule for the at-home pattern.** The message should
   claim a pause in completions and point to `/readyz` as a check, not assert a full room and
   Redis. A guard on recent completions or on session age could also be considered. Code
   ([`rules.ts`](../../src/shared/telemetry/rules.ts)) and the alert table in the runbook.
5. **Retry door messages on transient egress errors.** Today a timeout or `EAI_AGAIN` fails the
   send with no retry, and a repeat save is the only way to resend. A bounded retry for errors
   that carry no Meta code is the likely change. Code
   ([`admin.whatsapp-door.service.ts`](../../src/modules/admin/admin.whatsapp-door.service.ts)).
6. **Add the venue address to `TRUSTED_IPS` before doors open, with a pre-flight check.** The
   check should fail while the variable is empty or lacks the venue. Runbook.
7. **Export what the post-event record needs.** Include the `whatsapp_door_sends` table, and keep
   the standby's database before any cut-back decision. Runbook.
8. **Treat egress failures as a signal.** Four dependencies timed out on the same day with no
   alert. A cheap egress probe would separate a host fault from a venue fault. Code and runbook.
9. **Label alerts with the host.** Discord alerts carry no host, so the standby window cannot be
   read from them. Code.
10. **Record collections the counter did not see through the audited path.** The volunteer meal
    and lunch were set in `psql`, which leaves no `redemptions` row. A bulk _mark collected_ on
    `/admin/redemptions`, with its reason code, would have cleared the chips and kept the audit
    trail. Code, or a runbook step until it exists.

## Method

Sources are the roster and redemption export, `app_events`, the warn-level application log, the
Discord alert history, operator notes, and the post-event traffic summary. Figures come from one
script run over those files; nothing is estimated.

- Times are BRT (UTC−3). The export is in UTC and converted.
- `checkin_at` and some redemption times have minute resolution. Time-to-share figures are
  accurate to one minute.
- The log and `app_events` are filtered to warn-level and above, and to failed requests. Neither
  is a request count.
- Archived rows (9 participants, 1 volunteer) are excluded from every denominator.
- Operator facts, such as the standby window, the survey counts and the 19:00 deploy, are given
  as stated and are not corroborated by the files unless noted.
- Identifiers, addresses, ticket codes and people are left out.
