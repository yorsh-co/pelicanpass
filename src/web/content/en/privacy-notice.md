## What data the app uses

For each registered person, only three pieces of personal data:

- **First and last name** — provided by the person responsible for your
  registration for the event.
- **Phone number** — provided in the same registration.
- **Nickname** — you can type one yourself after checking in, if you want to
  change how your name appears on your screen.

If you are a **volunteer**, the app also uses **information about your service**
at the event, such as team, role and schedule, set by the volunteer
coordinators. It is **not included in the final report**.

Beyond these, the app records information that does not identify you on its
own: your **check-in code, number and time**, and your choices of **snack,
drink and T-shirt**.

When you collect one of these items, the app also records **the time of the
pickup** and **which volunteer served you**. If a pickup is not possible —
because the item was already collected, or because it was not part of your
registration — the attempt is recorded too. This is used to verify handovers and
to identify misuse of tickets, and nothing else. This record is destroyed along
with the server and is **not included in the final report**.

> Your date of birth, ID document, address, email, photo or any other personal
> data is **never processed by this app.**

## What each piece of data is used for

- **Confirming the right person came in** — the ticket code identifies your
  registration, the name lets you confirm on screen that the registration is
  yours, and the last four digits of the phone number are the code that
  completes the check-in. This is what stops someone else from using your
  ticket. Event volunteers can also check the name at the door.
- **Helping you at the door** — authorized volunteers can see your phone number,
  to fix a mistyped number or to help you complete the check-in if you do not
  remember which number you registered with.
- **Informing you about the event** — the phone number is the channel through
  which the check-in link and event notices reach you.
- **Personalizing your experience** — the nickname changes how your name appears
  to you inside the app.
- **Guiding volunteers** — the information about their service tells volunteers
  where and when they will serve, on the ticket and in WhatsApp messages.

> **Zero marketing**, **zero tracking** and **zero reuse**: the app has no
> advertising, uses no tracking pixel, and your data is not sold, cross-matched
> with other databases or used for anything outside the event.

## If you are under 18

Your name and phone number were provided by **your legal guardian** when you
were registered for the event, in the registration system. The consent that
authorizes this processing is theirs.

The guardian's data **does not enter** this app — unless they chose to provide
their own phone number instead of yours.

If the registration is done on the spot, on the day of the event, the same data
is collected at the time of registration and goes through the app in the same
way.

Inside the app, your main identifier is the six-character code on your ticket
and a random number generated by the database, not your name.

## Where the data is kept

Storage is 100% local, on a server belonging to the organizer.

- **Local database** — kept on a local server, on a closed network, with no open
  access from the internet. It is destroyed after the event.
- **Temporary cache** — during check-in, it holds the ticket code, the name and
  the first digits of the phone number, so you can confirm the registration is
  yours; this clears itself within 15 minutes. After check-in, it holds only a
  random identifier of your registration, so you do not have to repeat the
  process on every screen, and it clears itself after 24 hours.
- **Operation logs** — record the time, route and response code of each access,
  together with the network address (IP), the type of browser or device and the
  country the access came from. They are used to monitor the app's performance
  and to identify abuse, and nothing else: they feed no behavior analysis and
  no one's profile. Errors in the app, including those that happen in your
  browser, are recorded in the same way. **They do not record name, nickname or
  phone number.** They are destroyed along with the server.

## The platforms we use

Three, and only what the event needs to work:

- **Cloudflare** — handles the connection and protects the app against attacks.
  It receives the network address (IP) and technical connection data. To protect
  the app, the screens **pass through Cloudflare** on their way to your device,
  but it does not keep or use this content for anything other than delivering
  it. On the check-in screen, Cloudflare also runs an automatic check to tell
  people from robots. For volunteers, the email used to sign in to the admin
  panel is kept on Cloudflare's access list, along with the record of when each
  person signed in.
- **WhatsApp (Meta)** — used to send the check-in information. It receives your
  **phone number**, your **first name**, your **ticket code** and your **ticket
  link**. If you are a **volunteer**, it also receives **information about your
  service** at the event, such as team and schedule. Nothing beyond that. Meta
  tells the app whether the message was delivered and read, and this is
  recorded. Replies sent to that number are neither read nor stored. Meta
  deletes message and user data after
  [30 days](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/data-privacy-and-security/#store-collected-data).
- **Tailscale** — creates the private network (VPN) that serves as a backup path
  for event volunteers to reach the admin panel. It receives only technical
  connection data from the devices on that network: device name, operating
  system, network address (IP), public key and access times. The participant
  data that appears in the panel — name, ticket code and phone number — travels
  over this connection **end-to-end encrypted**, between the organizer's server
  and the volunteer's device, and Tailscale **has no way to read it**.

> Cloudflare and Meta are United States companies, certified under the **Data
> Privacy Framework**. Tailscale is a Canadian company, and its contract adopts
> the **European Commission's standard contractual clauses** (GDPR).

## Cookies and browser preferences

The app uses **session cookies**, which page scripts cannot access and which are
deleted when you close the browser. They keep you signed in during and after
check-in, so you do not have to repeat the process on every screen. Because they
are necessary for the app to work, the app's screens show a simple notice.

**Cloudflare** also sets its own cookies in your browser, to tell people from
robots and to protect the app against attacks. They do not identify you and are
not read by the app.

The app also stores **usage preferences**, such as the light or dark theme you
choose with the button on the screen and the language of the screens. The theme
stays only on your own device and is not sent to the server. The language is kept
in a cookie called **locale**, sent to the server so that the screens come in
the language you chose; it contains only the language code (for example,
`pt-BR`) and lasts one year. None of these preferences identifies you or is used
for tracking, and all of them disappear when you clear your browser data.

> There is no social network pixel, no behavior analytics tool (other than
> Cloudflare itself) and no tracker of any kind.

## How long the data is kept

- **During the event** — you use the app to collect your T-shirt, get your snack,
  see event information and answer the feedback form. The only personal data
  that appears on these screens is your first name and, on a volunteer's ticket,
  the information about their service.
- **After the event** — the feedback form remains available, on the same device
  where you checked in, until the closing time announced by the organizers.
- **Up to 24 hours later** — the server is shut down and the database is
  destroyed along with it. Any backup made before or during the event is
  destroyed too.

When you answer the form, the app keeps only the fact that you have already
answered, so that it is not submitted twice. This record is deleted before the
final report and is never linked to your answers.

Only two files survive: a **pseudonymized attendance report** and the
**anonymized feedback form answers**, with no name, ticket, phone number or time
that would link an answer to the person who gave it.

> The report keeps the ticket code, in case a check-in needs to be verified
> against the data in the event's registration system. Alongside it are whether
> you are a participant or a volunteer, your check-in number and time, the items
> in your registration, whether and when you collected each one, and whether the
> registration was canceled. **Name, nickname, phone number and the information
> about volunteer service are not in the report**, and neither is the record of
> which volunteer served you.

## Your rights

At any time, you or your guardian can ask to:

- find out whether we process your data, and which data it is;
- correct anything that is wrong;
- delete your data from our database.

> Important information: if you ask for your data to be removed **before** the
> event, you will not be able to check in through the app, and entry will have to
> be handled manually at the door. And **after** the server is shut down there is
> nothing left to delete — the data has already been destroyed.
